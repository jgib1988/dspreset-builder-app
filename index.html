Decent Sampler Preset Builder

How to use

This page builds a Decent Sampler preset (.dspreset) from a folder of WAV samples. Your audio stays on your computer: it is read in your browser and never uploaded. Only the preset file is saved.

Name your samples Name_Note_vN_rrN.wav, e.g. Piano_C3_v1_rr1.wav. The note is a name (C3, F#2, Bb1) or a MIDI number (0–127), with middle C = C3 (MIDI 60). v is the velocity layer and rr the round-robin, each counted from 1. Files that don't match are flagged and left out, unless you click Assign in the file table to give them a name, note, layer and round-robin by hand.

  1. Click Pick sample folder and choose the folder your samples are in.
  2. Check the Samples and Mapping tabs.
  3. Optionally set the sound, modulation and plugin controls on the Sound, Modulation and Controls tabs.
  4. Click Export .dspreset. It downloads (usually to your Downloads folder). The Export tab lists anything that needs fixing first.
  5. Move the .dspreset into the same folder as the samples, then open it in Decent Sampler.
  • Presets need Decent Sampler 1.34.0 or later. Some effects, such as the filter types, behave differently in older versions.
  • Chrome or Edge give the best folder picker. Other browsers work too, but the folder dialog looks different.
  • Click a section's heading to fold it away.
  • Mix layers play several sample names together on the same keys (e.g. close and room mics), each with its own volume knob in the plugin's Mixer panel.
  • Sequences are patterns the instrument plays by itself. Pressing a trigger key (C-1 to B-1 by default) starts one, transposed by that key. Trigger keys don't play samples. A Play button or Play menu (in Controls) plays one from the plugin window instead, with no key held.
  • The Arpeggiator plays held notes one at a time in a pattern. While it is on, every note goes through it, including sequence notes.
  • Save project keeps your work as a .json file. To carry on later, load it, then pick the sample folder again.

You can also drop a .dsproject.json file anywhere on this page to load it.

Mapping

Drag a zone to move it, or drag its edges to resize it. Shift-click zones (or Select all) to set trim, loop, volume, pan or tuning on all of them at once. Re-picking the folder keeps the mapping and loops of files that are still there.

Release samples

Tick the sample names that should play when a key is released (e.g. a piano's damper sound). They get their own mapping: pick "Key up" above the grid to see and edit it.

Key switches

Give sample names an articulation, then pick a key for each articulation: pressing that key switches to it (the key itself doesn't play). Each articulation gets its own mapping: pick it above the grid.

Mix layers

Give sample names a mix layer to play them together on the same keys, e.g. close and room mics. Each mix layer gets its own mapping: pick it above the grid.

Choking and voice limits

Tick which sounds cut each sound off, e.g. HatOpen is cut off by HatClosed. It can be one-way: tick the reverse too to make them cut each other off. Ticking a sound's own name makes a new hit stop its previous one. A voice limit caps how many notes of that sound ring at once.

Per-note effects

Each note gets its own copy of these, so an envelope or velocity can sweep each note's filter on its own. The Effects above act on the whole instrument's mixed sound instead. Only filters, gain and chorus work per note.

Modulators

Sequences

Patterns the instrument plays by itself when you press a trigger key, using its own samples. The pattern follows the DAW's tempo.

Arpeggiator

Plays the notes you hold one at a time, in a pattern, across octaves. Needs Decent Sampler 1.26 or later.
